Time to break the blogging drought with a hands-on post.

For a recent product launch, our team used mind mapping to build the information architecture (IA), rather than the more conventional site-tree approach.

> "Information architecture is the practice of deciding how to arrange the parts of something to be understandable." - IA Institute

We used software to run the process. Ideally we'd have collaborated in a physical space, but since that wasn't an option, we went with what felt like the next best thing.

[MindNode 2](https://mindnode.com/mindnode/mac) - a user-friendly Mac App Store tool - became our primary way of visualising early ideas. It let us collaborate effectively as a team and share progress with stakeholders and users alike.

With MindNode, you can quickly add nodes as new information or topics emerge - from user research, search demand, stakeholder conversations, or input from subject matter experts.

## Diagram tips

A few things that helped us build clearer IA diagrams:

- **Line colours.** Use different colours to separate topics or highlight different audiences.
- **Line weightings.** Adjust thickness to signal the volume or complexity of information.
- **Arrows.** Show relationships between nodes, or flag important cross-linking.
- **Node types.** Use distinct node styles to represent content type (video, image, text).
- **Notes.** Add short explanations of where information came from - user research findings, for example.

It also helps to create a couple of "legend" nodes as a quick reference for anyone viewing the diagram.

MindNode is flexible enough to handle change easily - move or reorganise elements and everything else adjusts and resizes around it. It's intentionally loose enough that no one mistakes it for a final deliverable, while still being clear and genuinely collaborative.

For more on IA more broadly, [The Ultimate Guide to Information Architecture](https://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2015/02/the-ultimate-guide-to-information-architecture/) on Web Designer Depot is a solid reference.

Give it a try, and let me know how you get on.
